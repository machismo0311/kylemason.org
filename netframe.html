Featured work

Making the internet fault-tolerant

Problem

A single ISP link was a single point of failure for a cluster running DNS, Kubernetes, monitoring, and backups. If the line dropped, everything went dark.

Design

I added a hardwired 5G cellular backup as a second WAN on the OPNsense firewall, with health-based gateway monitoring for automatic failover in under ten seconds and no manual intervention.

The catch

Before touching production, I verified the live firewall configuration through its API and found a leftover subnet alias that would have silently collided with the backup link. I confirmed it was unused through ARP and DHCP checks, then chose the low-risk fix. Measuring twice is what separates a clean change from an outage.

Result

Automatic sub-ten-second failover, upload throughput at near parity with the primary line, zero manual steps, documented as a runbook in git. The next iteration removes the router itself as a single point of failure with a CARP high-availability pair.

The system

Architecture at a glance

  • NetworkOPNsense firewall, Juniper EX3400 core switch, seven VLANs, self-hosted Headscale mesh VPN.
  • ComputeSeven Proxmox nodes, including two Dell R730 GPU servers for ML research and LLM inference.
  • StorageZFS on a 24-bay SuperMicro plus a NetApp shelf, with Proxmox Backup Server.
  • KubernetesThree-node HA RKE2 with Cilium, MetalLB, and a private registry on internal TLS.
  • SecurityVLAN segmentation, out-of-band isolation, a Wazuh SIEM, and an internal certificate authority.
  • ObservabilityPrometheus and Grafana with alerting to chat, including dead-man's-switch watchdogs.
